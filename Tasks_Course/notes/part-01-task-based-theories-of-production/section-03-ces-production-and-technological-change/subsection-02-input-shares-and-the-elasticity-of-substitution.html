    Input shares and the elasticity of substitution¶

    As we hinted at in the discussion of the Cobb--Douglas technology, the elasticity of substitution also determines how the division of income between capital and labor responds to changes in relative prices. When \(\sigma=1\), the shares are constant. When \(\sigma\neq1\), they change.

    For cost minimization, let \(\lambda\) denote the multiplier on the output constraint. The first-order conditions are \(r=\lambda\,\partial Y/\partial K\) and \(w=\lambda\,\partial Y/\partial L\). Under constant returns and perfect competition, \(\lambda=p\). Using the marginal products derived above gives

    \[\begin{align} rK &\;=\; pz^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}}\alpha \left(A_K K\right)^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}} Y^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}, \tag{3.11}\\ wL &\;=\; pz^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}}(1-\alpha) \left(A_L L\right)^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}}Y^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}. \tag{3.12} \end{align}\]

    Dividing each payment by total cost cancels the common factor \(pz^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}}Y^{1/\sigma}\):

    \[\begin{align} \alpha_K &\;\equiv\; \frac{rK}{rK+wL} \;=\;\frac{\alpha (A_K K)^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}}} {\alpha (A_K K)^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}}+(1-\alpha)(A_L L)^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}}}, \tag{3.13}\\ \alpha_L &\;\equiv\; \frac{wL}{rK+wL} \;=\; \frac{(1-\alpha) \left(A_L L\right)^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}}}{\alpha \left(A_K K \right)^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}}+(1-\alpha) \left(A_L L\right)^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}}}. \tag{3.14} \end{align}\]

    To express the shares in terms of prices and technologies, first take their ratio and use the MRT condition:

    \[\begin{align} \frac{\alpha_K}{\alpha_L} &=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha} \left(\frac{A_KK}{A_LL}\right)^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}}, \quad\text{where the MRT gives}\; \frac{A_KK}{A_LL} =\left[\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\frac{A_K}{A_L}\frac{w}{r}\right]^\sigma. \tag{3.15} \end{align}\]

    Substituting the second equation into the first gives

    \[\begin{align} \frac{\alpha_K}{\alpha_L} &\;=\;\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha} \left[\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\frac{A_K}{A_L}\frac{w}{r}\right]^{\sigma-1} \;=\;\left(\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)^\sigma \left(\frac{r/A_K}{w/A_L}\right)^{1-\sigma}. \tag{3.16} \end{align}\]

    Since \(\alpha_K+\alpha_L=1\), the individual shares are

    \[\begin{align} \alpha_K &=\frac{\alpha^\sigma(r/A_K)^{1-\sigma}} {\alpha^\sigma(r/A_K)^{1-\sigma}+(1-\alpha)^\sigma(w/A_L)^{1-\sigma}}, \tag{3.17}\\ \alpha_L &=\frac{(1-\alpha)^\sigma(w/A_L)^{1-\sigma}} {\alpha^\sigma(r/A_K)^{1-\sigma}+(1-\alpha)^\sigma(w/A_L)^{1-\sigma}}. \tag{3.18} \end{align}\]

    At \(\sigma=1\), we recover \(\alpha_K=\alpha\) and \(\alpha_L=1-\alpha\).

    These expressions are useful because they tell us about how inequality in income between input factors changes with relative prices and with technology:

    \[\begin{equation} \log\left(\frac{\alpha_K}{\alpha_L}\right) \;=\; \sigma\log\left(\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right) \;+\;(1-\sigma) \left[ \log\left(\frac{r}{w}\right) -\log\left(\frac{A_K}{A_L}\right) \right]. \tag{3.19} \end{equation}\]

    Holding technology fixed, the relative-share response is

    \[\begin{equation} \dd\log\frac{\alpha_K}{\alpha_L} \;=\;(1-\sigma)\dd\log\frac{r}{w}. \tag{3.20} \end{equation}\]

    A fall in \(r/w\) raises capital's share when \(\sigma>1\) and lowers it when \(\sigma<1\). A change in technology has the opposite effect as being more productive is equivalent to being cheaper.

    \[\begin{equation} \dd\log\frac{\alpha_K}{\alpha_L} \;=\;(\sigma-1)\dd\log\frac{A_K}{A_L}. \tag{3.21} \end{equation}\]
