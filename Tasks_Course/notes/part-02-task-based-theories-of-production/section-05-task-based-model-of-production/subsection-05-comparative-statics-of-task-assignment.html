    Part 2 / Section 5: Task-Based Model of Production Section 5.5

    Comparative statics of task assignment¶

    We can now look at how the firm's cost-minimizing choices respond to the relative input price \(w/r\). This response is summarized by the change in the assignment cutoff \(I\), which satisfies \(a_L(I)/a_K(I)=w/r\). Taking logs and differentiating gives

    \[\begin{equation} \left[\frac{a_L'(I)}{a_L(I)}-\frac{a_K'(I)}{a_K(I)}\right]\dd I \;=\;\dd\log(w/r) \quad\longrightarrow\quad \frac{\dd I}{\dd\log(w/r)} \;=\;\frac{1}{\dfrac{a_L'(I)}{a_L(I)}-\dfrac{a_K'(I)}{a_K(I)}}\;>\;0. \tag{5.20} \end{equation}\]

    The denominator is positive because labor's relative productivity increases with the task index. A higher wage relative to the rental rate therefore expands the machine bundle and contracts the human bundle. The response is larger when comparative advantage changes less sharply around the cutoff.

    We can also express this result using the elasticity of each productivity schedule with respect to the task index:

    \[\begin{equation} \varepsilon_j(i)\;\equiv\;\frac{\dd\log a_j(i)}{\dd\log i} \;=\;\frac{i\,a_j'(i)}{a_j(i)},\qquad j\in\{K,L\}, \qquad \frac{\dd\log I}{\dd\log(w/r)} \;=\;\frac{1}{\varepsilon_L(I)-\varepsilon_K(I)}. \tag{5.21} \end{equation}\]

    Under our assumptions, \(\varepsilon_L(I)>0\) and \(\varepsilon_K(I)<0\), so the gap in these elasticities is positive.

    This result is particularly useful to characterize how the aggregate demand for capital and labor reacts to changes in prices.

    How does reassignment affect substitution between the aggregate inputs?

    Dividing the input demands in (5.16) cancels their common output and price terms:

    \[\begin{equation} \frac{K}{L}\;=\;\left(\frac{w}{r}\right)^\eta\frac{H_K(I)}{H_L(I)}. \tag{5.22} \end{equation}\]

    The limits of integration imply \(H_K'(I)=a_K(I)^{\eta-1}\) and \(H_L'(I)=-a_L(I)^{\eta-1}\). We use this to get the elasticity of substitution with task assignment:

    \[\begin{align} \sigma\;\equiv\;\frac{\dd\log(K/L)}{\dd\log(w/r)} &\;=\;\underbrace{\eta}_{\text{fixed assignment}} \;+\;\underbrace{\left[ \frac{a_K(I)^{\eta-1}}{H_K(I)} +\frac{a_L(I)^{\eta-1}}{H_L(I)} \right]\frac{\dd I}{\dd\log(w/r)}}_{\text{task reassignment}}, \tag{5.23}\\ &\;=\;\eta+ \frac{I}{\varepsilon_L(I)-\varepsilon_K(I)} \left[\frac{a_K(I)^{\eta-1}}{H_K(I)} +\frac{a_L(I)^{\eta-1}}{H_L(I)}\right]\;>\;\eta. \nonumber \end{align}\]

    At a fixed assignment, the firm substitutes between the quantities of machine-produced and human-produced tasks with elasticity \(\eta\). Allowing assignment to change adds a positive response: capital takes over marginal tasks previously performed by labor. Thus aggregate inputs are more substitutable than tasks at an interior cutoff, although \(\sigma\) need not be constant or exceed one when \(\eta<1\).

    For example, with Cobb--Douglas task aggregation, \(\eta=1\), we have \(H_K(I)=I\) and \(H_L(I)=1-I\), so

    \[\begin{equation} \sigma\;=\;1+\frac{1}{(1-I)}\frac{\dd \log I}{\dd\log(w/r)}. \tag{5.24} \end{equation}\]

    The productivity schedules in Section 6.1, after relabeling its two labor inputs as capital and labor, imply \(\dd \log I/\dd\log(w/r)=\kappa (1-I)\) and hence \(\sigma=1+\kappa\). Section 6.2 instead shows how reassignment can raise an across-task elasticity \(\eta<1\) to an aggregate elasticity of exactly one.

    The same reasoning applies to study changes in technology. Write task productivity as

    \[\begin{equation} a_K(i)=\overline{a}_K\alpha_K(i),\qquad a_L(i)=\overline{a}_L\alpha_L(i), \tag{5.25} \end{equation}\]

    where \(\overline{a}_K,\overline{a}_L>0\) scale productivity across all tasks and the fixed schedules \(\alpha_K(i),\alpha_L(i)\) describe its task-specific pattern. The cutoff condition becomes

    \[\begin{equation} \frac{\alpha_L(I)}{\alpha_K(I)} \;=\;\frac{w}{r}\frac{\overline{a}_K}{\overline{a}_L} \quad\longrightarrow\quad \big[\varepsilon_L(I)-\varepsilon_K(I)\big]\dd\log I \;=\;\dd\log(w/r)+\dd\log(\overline{a}_K/\overline{a}_L). \tag{5.26} \end{equation}\]

    Multiplying a productivity schedule by a constant does not change its elasticity with respect to the task index, so the same \(\varepsilon_j(I)\) apply. Holding \(w/r\) fixed therefore gives

    \[\begin{equation} \left.\frac{\dd\log I}{\dd\log(\overline{a}_K/\overline{a}_L)}\right|_{w/r} \;=\;\frac{1}{\varepsilon_L(I)-\varepsilon_K(I)}\;>\;0. \tag{5.27} \end{equation}\]

    An increase in \(\overline{a}_K\) lowers capital's cost of performing every task and expands the machine bundle. An increase in \(\overline{a}_L\) has the opposite effect. For assignment, a proportional increase in \(\overline{a}_K/\overline{a}_L\) has the same effect as the same proportional increase in \(w/r\).
